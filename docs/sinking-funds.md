# Sinking Funds

Sinking Funds are planned future expenses that you save toward over time. Unlike debts (owed to others), sinking funds are "debts to yourself" — things important enough to roadmap and budget for, even though they slow down other financial goals.

## Examples

- Replacing a roof or fence
- Buying furniture (couch, mattress, desk)
- Vehicle maintenance or replacement
- Appliance replacement
- Medical procedures
- Education expenses

## How It Works

### Lifecycle

Each sinking fund moves through a status lifecycle:

1. **Planning** — You've identified the need and estimated the cost
2. **Saving** — You're actively contributing monthly (auto-transitions on first contribution)
3. **Ready** — Fully funded, ready to purchase (auto-transitions when current >= target)
4. **Purchased** — Money spent, item acquired
5. **Completed** — Done

### Priority Levels

- **Critical** — Urgent need, consequences if delayed (e.g., leaking roof)
- **High** — Important, should be next in line
- **Medium** — Standard planned expense
- **Low** — Nice to have, can wait

## Cash Waterfall Integration

Sinking funds have their own tier in the Cash Waterfall allocation system, positioned between debt payments and owner's draw:

1. Operating Reserve
2. Tax Reserve
3. IRS Installment
4. Debt Payments
5. **Sinking Funds** (monthly contributions)
6. Owner's Draw
7. Investment

The total monthly contributions across all active sinking funds are allocated from incoming revenue.

## Navigation

Sinking Funds has its own page at `/life/sinking-funds`, accessible from:
- The sidebar navigation (piggy bank icon)
- The Life section sub-nav bar (between Debts and Budget)

## Features

- Create, edit, and delete sinking funds
- Record contributions with dates and notes
- Progress tracking with percentage and ETA
- Auto-status transitions (planning → saving → ready)
- Stats dashboard (total target, total saved, monthly contributions, overall progress)
- Completed funds archive
